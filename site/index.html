How to read these stats

PF/G & PA/G are points scored and allowed per game; soccer uses GF/G & GA/G for goals. Diff/G is raw margin. Cap Diff/G caps each game at ±21 points in flag or ±3 goals in soccer, then averages.

SOS is opponents’ average win rate excluding every game against the team being evaluated. Ties count as half a win; repeat opponents count once per meeting. Coverage shows the share of meetings with usable opponent history. Partial coverage means SOS is incomplete.

Adj Diff/G (experimental) adds each opponent’s external capped margin per game to that game’s capped margin, shrinking the opponent adjustment by n/(n+3), where n is the opponent’s games against other teams. It is shown only at 100% opponent-history coverage. The three-game shrinkage is an editorial choice, not fitted to SBMSA results. This is not a calibrated prediction.

Flag: shutout rate is the share of games allowing zero points; one-score record covers final margins of 8 points or less, including ties. Soccer: clean-sheet rate is the share allowing zero goals, including 0–0 draws; points per game uses 3 for a win and 1 for a draw as a comparison convention, not a claim about league tiebreak rules.

Division strength: no cross-division adjustment without verified cross-division games. Opponent metrics use each published division independently. Disconnected schedules cannot establish which division is strongest. No xG, EPA or calibrated expected-win probabilities are inferred from final scores.

Methodology, scope & official sources

Independent, provisional analysis. Not official SBMSA standings. Teams with completed games are ranked by (wins + 0.5 × ties) / games played, then average scoring margin per game. The default caps each individual game at ±21 points for football or ±3 goals for soccer before averaging. Raw mode averages uncapped game margins. Caps are editorial choices, not league rules.

Equal win rates and average margins retain competition ranks (1, 1, 3…). Numerical differences within 0.000000001 are treated as ties. Alphabetical order inside a tie is not a tiebreaker. Unequal games played are handled with per-game rates; small samples remain uncertain. No roster, individual-player, prior-season, or opponent-strength adjustments are used. Soccer is ranked by the same method as soon as scores are published.
