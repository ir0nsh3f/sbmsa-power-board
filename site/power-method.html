Opponent-adjusted power

Method: opponent-ridge-margin-v2. Higher is stronger. W–L–T describes results but never breaks a power tie or drives ordering. This replaces the former win-rate-first board, not an additional standings option.

Exact model

For each completed game g:
  y_g = clip(home_score − away_score, −cap, +cap)

Choose all team ratings r jointly to minimize:
  ½ Σ_g [y_g − (r_home − r_away)]² + (3/2) Σ_team r_team²

cap = 21 points (JV Flag), 3 goals (8U / 6U soccer)
Home advantage = 0. Every completed game has weight 1.
No time decay, roster data, prior-season rating, or win-rate term.

Fit each sport/age independently. A rating is a points/goals strength effect, not that team’s average margin. A rating difference estimates the capped-margin target between connected teams under this model—not an uncapped betting spread, scoring forecast, or win probability. Ratings should not be compared between sports or ages.

The zero-centered penalty is equivalent to three unit-weight pseudo-observations of each team’s rating being zero. Under a Gaussian working model with residual variance σ², the independent prior variance is σ²/3; no numerical σ or calibrated uncertainty is asserted. The fixed strength 3 and caps are transparent editorial assumptions, not tuned or validated forecasts. Early samples shrink strongly toward zero. Larger samples increasingly determine the fit.

Opponent adjustment is simultaneous: the normal equations give r_i = (sum of the team’s capped game margins + sum of opponents’ current ratings)/(GP_i + 3). Beating a stronger opponent earns more credit; losing to a weaker opponent costs more, all else equal. Opponent strength is resolved jointly across the result network, not a one-pass average of records.

Blowouts, ties and reproducibility

Capping the response bounds each game’s score contribution: a 40-point flag win contributes exactly the same target as a 21-point win. The quadratic loss is applied after clipping; this is not a Huber residual fit. No uncapped power mode is offered. The existing Cap/Raw control changes only the descriptive Margin/G column, not any power rating, rank, spotlight, schedule badge, or historical capture.

Both pure JavaScript and standard-library Python start at zero, sort team identities and games deterministically, and apply simultaneous Jacobi iterations. Stop when the maximum rating change is ≤10⁻¹²; fail rather than publish a partial fit after 10,000 iterations. The positive ridge makes the objective strictly convex and the iteration a contraction. Tests reconcile both implementations and check normal-equation residuals.

For ranking, round power to 8 decimal places using floor(power × 10⁸ + 0.5)/10⁸, descending. Equal values share competition ranks (1, 1, 3); team name then division gives stable display order only. Display rounds to 2 decimals, so visually equal values can have distinct ranks. GP=0 is unrated, not last; missing completed-game evidence cannot create a rating.

Disconnected schedules: a real limitation

Overall league-wide comparisons are provisional. Without games connecting two result groups, scores cannot identify their relative strength. This can happen across divisions and within a division early in the season. Ridge places every disconnected group at a zero mean by assumption; it does not discover that the groups are equally strong or calibrate divisions. Within a connected group, comparative ratings are supported by results, still with early-sample uncertainty. A division filter retains overall ranks, not invented local calibration.

The board reports result-group count. Schedule ranks and Top matchup badges use these same provisional full sport/age power ranks. Top matchup still requires both teams at least 3 GP and both in the rounded-up top quarter of all rated teams (including 1–2 GP teams in the denominator). Ties expand the cutoff. Close records remains a descriptive record comparison, not a prediction.

History and separate forecasts

Immutable old captures retain win-rate-capped-margin-v1 and their original bytes. History index schema 2 identifies each capture’s method, with a method registry. The first accepted source check under this method appends a new-method baseline even if raw results are unchanged, labelled ranking-method-change. This is not rank movement. Any future chart must split series at method boundaries; do not connect, subtract ranks, or carry a weekly observation across that boundary. No new ranking is backdated onto old results.
